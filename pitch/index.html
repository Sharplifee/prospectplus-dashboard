The problem is not a shortage of records. It is that nobody reads them.

A homeowner's situation changes on paper long before it changes on the MLS. A lien is recorded. A parent dies and an heir is named. A spouse is removed from title. A lender substitutes a trustee. These are public documents, filed with the county, dated and numbered.

They are also nearly unusable. Each county runs its own system. A notice of default names the borrower as the grantee, not the grantor. Trustee sales are published in newspapers, not recorded. The same person appears as "SMITH, JOHN A", "SMITH JOHN A TEE" and "JOHN A SMITH ET AL" across three filings. The tax roll packs owner, parcel and two dollar figures into a single line of text.

So the industry waits for the listing, and then five agents call the same house on the same afternoon. The listing was decided weeks earlier, over a kitchen table, by someone who called a friend.

Premier Prospect exists to be in the room before that phone call. It reads every instrument the county records, resolves the names into people, joins them to parcels and tax history, scores how much the evidence converges, and hands an agent a name, a reason, and a document number they can look up themselves.

How a name becomes a lead

The pipeline runs unattended every night. Each stage has to pass before the next one runs.

Collect

County recorder indexes, district court calendars, the delinquent tax roll and the assessor are read on their own schedules. Each record keeps its entry number, date and county, so anything the system says can be traced back to a document.

Resolve

Name variants collapse into one owner. Trusts held by a family stay in; banks, LLCs, builders, cities and HOAs are removed at the door. Death affidavits are re-keyed to the living heir, because the heir is the one who will sell.

Confirm

An owner only qualifies for the top tier when the name is tied to real property: a recorded document, a parcel serial, a tax roll line, or the assessor's file. A name that appears only on a court calendar is capped, however alarming the case looks.

Score

The strongest single signal anchors the score. Independent corroboration raises it. Evidence decays as it ages, clusters of filings inside thirty days raise it, and a recorded sale or trustee's deed retires it. Every lead carries a plain-English reason.

Prove

Before anything is published, an integrity gate checks the invariants and re-reads a random sample of top-tier documents from the county's own website. If the document on the page does not match the label in the system, the run fails and nothing ships.

Court and estate layers average under three weeks old. Their rates are reported as zero rather than hidden, and they will be re-measured nightly as deeds land.

Integrity is enforced, not audited

Lead systems usually look clean when someone checks them. This one refuses to be dirty in the first place.

The database rejects bad rows before they are written: a recorder source cannot emit a trustee sale, a placeholder name cannot enter, a county label must match its source, and an outcome can only be logged by a registered agent. There is no test data in production and no sample data on any screen; empty sections say they are empty.

After every nightly score, the gate checks that every top-tier lead is property-confirmed, that no lender or trust sits in the queue as a homeowner, that every buyer's purchase count equals distinct recorded deeds, that every registered source is still producing, and that a random sample of top-tier documents still says on the county's website what the system says it says.

Ten gated runs so far. Nine passed. The one that failed caught four comparable sales labeled with the wrong county on the first night, which is exactly what the gate is for.

The buyer side and the seller side are the same engine reading the same documents. When a seller lead retires because its deed was recorded, the grantee on that deed becomes a buyer profile the same night.

Why this side matters to a listing

A seller in distress does not want a valuation. They want to know the house will sell, quickly, to someone real. Walking into that conversation with the names of the people who have bought three homes in that county this year, and whether they paid cash, is a different conversation from walking in with a comparable sales report.

The system pairs its highest-conviction sellers against buyers in the same county, weighted by how recently and how often the buyer has transacted. Price bands are shown only when the county provides enough to state one; where it does not, the app says "not yet resolved" rather than inventing a number.

What we do not claim on this side

Buyer profiles carry names, counties, dates and document numbers. They do not yet carry phone numbers or email; that is the same enrichment step that gates the seller launch. Cash-versus-financed is derived from whether a deed of trust was recorded on the same entry, and is reported as unresolved where the county index does not show it. Nothing on this side is scored from web traffic, form fills, or purchased intent data.
